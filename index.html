How it works

From inbox to invoice, in four steps.

The AI inside

It reads orders the way your best dispatcher does.

No templates to set up and no new inbox. Kalyd works on the emails you already get.

  • It reads any order. Email body or PDF attachment, in Dutch or English.
  • It builds the order. Parties, loading reference, addresses, dates, pallets and price, filled in for you.
  • It spots dangerous goods. ADR loads are flagged automatically, so the right driver and truck get assigned.
  • You stay in control. Unclear fields are highlighted for a quick check before anything reaches a driver.

How much does Kalyd cost?

Pricing depends on your fleet size and order volume. Start with a free pilot on your real orders, and we'll agree a price that fits your operation.

Do we need to change our email or set up a new inbox?

No. Kalyd works on the emails you already get. Your customers keep sending orders to the same address, and nothing changes on their side.

Can we import our customers, drivers and trucks from Excel or another system?

Yes. Send us your data as an Excel or CSV file and our team will load it for you during your pilot, so you can start working with your real customers, drivers and trucks from day one.

What happens if the AI reads an order wrong?

Nothing goes out on its own. Every order Kalyd reads lands on your dispatch board, where your team can review it and fix any field in seconds before assigning a driver.

Which order formats can Kalyd read?

Email text and PDF attachments, in Dutch or English. No templates to set up: Kalyd reads each order as it arrives.

Do drivers need to install an app?

No. Jobs go out on WhatsApp, with no app to install and no login. Drivers use the tool they already have on their phone.

Does Kalyd support Peppol e-invoicing?

Yes. You can send invoices through Peppol straight from a dispatched order, with VAT included.

What fleet size is Kalyd built for?

Kalyd is built for small and mid-sized road freight companies, from a handful of trucks to larger fleets. It was shaped inside a working transport company, so it fits how real dispatch teams run. Each company gets its own isolated database and login.
